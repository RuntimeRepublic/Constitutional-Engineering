## 1. Twelve-question test (apply per material cost later)

1. What exact **function** does the expenditure perform?  
2. What **causes** the cost to exist?  
3. Is the function required for the **underlying lottery transaction**?  
4. Is it required by constitution, statute, regulation, contract, or another **authority**?  
5. If legally required, can the underlying authority itself **lawfully be changed** by Bill A?  
6. Is the cost **external/direct** or **governmental/administrative**?  
7. Could software or automation perform **part or all** of the function?  
8. Could competitive procurement **materially reduce** it?  
9. Could the process be eliminated through **architecture redesign**?  
10. Does elimination of the cost **stop the lottery transaction** or merely stop the current way government administers it?  
11. Is there a **less expensive method** of satisfying the same legitimate requirement?  
12. Is the claimed cost supported by **controlled evidence**?

## 2. Candidate outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning (Definition-level) |
|---|---|
| **UNAVOIDABLE DIRECT ECONOMIC COST** | Evidenced as materially necessary under WD-BILL-A-165 |
| **CONTROLLABLE ADMINISTRATION** | Evidenced as potentially engineerable downward under WD-BILL-A-166 |
| **PROTECTED TRANSITION OBLIGATION** | Temporary legal/contractual protection; not permanent necessity (WD-BILL-A-178) |
| **POTENTIALLY REDESIGNABLE** | Function may remain; method may change |
| **POTENTIALLY ELIMINABLE** | Function itself may not be required under Bill A architecture |
| **UNKNOWN / RESEARCH REQUIRED** | Default until evidence exists |

Do **not** classify unsupported costs as unavoidable. Do **not** classify unsupported costs as controllable. Appearing on the cost-universe list (WD-BILL-A-172) **≠** classification.

Relation to CWC-CE-167 available-revenue test (WD-BILL-A-148): that test asks whether a **post-prize dollar** is available. This test asks how a **cost** is classified for the 90/10 base. Both remain frameworks. Neither is a current-law conclusion.
